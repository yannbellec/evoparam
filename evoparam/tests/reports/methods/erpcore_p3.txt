Evoked responses were analysed with evoparam (version {version}) using the preset 'erp' v1.0; validation: pending: ERP CORE (REQ-274). Each trial and channel was baseline-corrected by subtracting the mean of -200 to 0 ms of its own trial. Trials were averaged per channel (mean). In the window 50 to 700 ms, each waveform was modelled as a constant background plus up to 6 components (gaussian and skewed gaussian; full width at half maximum 10 to 350 ms, both polarities). Amplitudes and background coefficients were solved by variable projection and the other parameters by bounded least squares (linear loss, 3 starts). Components were added one at a time and kept when the BIC decreased by at least 10, computed on residuals whitened by an autoregressive model of the noise of the plus-minus average of the trials (alternating signs, whole epoch). A final model holding two overlapping components of opposite sign was refitted from starts on the extrema of the data and replaced when the refit was better; a component of the refitted model that no longer lowered the criterion by the same amount was removed. Uncertainty: Jacobian-based (Wald) intervals at 95 %. Onset and offset are the times where a component reaches 10 % of its peak amplitude. Components were labelled by latency window: P1 (80 to 130 ms, positive), N1 (130 to 200 ms, negative), N170 (130 to 200 ms, negative), P2 (150 to 270 ms, positive), P3 (250 to 600 ms, positive) and N400 (300 to 500 ms, negative). Classical measures (peak, mean amplitude, area and fractional-area latencies) were computed on the same window. The presence of a response in 50 to 700 ms was tested on every channel with a sign-flip permutation test of the trials (max_t statistic, 1000 permutations) against the baseline -200 to 0 ms (both polarities, alpha = 0.05, Benjamini-Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction across channels). Random draws used the seed 0; the result can be regenerated from its recorded provenance with evoparam.reproduce. Software: Bellec Y.V. evoparam: parameterizing evoked neural responses (version 0.1.0). https://github.com/yannbellec/evoparam Methods: Benjamini and Hochberg (1995) for the false-discovery-rate correction.
