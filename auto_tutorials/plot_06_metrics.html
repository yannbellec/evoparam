06: Metrics and model evaluation¶

Judge a fit: how well it follows the curve, how sure its numbers are, and what it warns.

Introduction¶

A fit always gives numbers. Before using them, three questions:

  1. does the model follow the curve, and is what is left over only noise?

  2. how sure is each number?

  3. did the fit warn about something?

evoparam answers each one: fit.gof (goodness of fit), the uncertainty of each bump (ci), and fit.status with fit.warnings.

# Import what we need
from evoparam import fit_response, gen_response

The scores of the fit¶

fit.gof holds the scores, all computed on the fit window:

  • r2: the share of the curve’s ups and downs that the model explains, from 0 (none) to 1 (all); adj_r2 is the same with a cost for each number of the model;

  • rmse: the typical size of what is left over (data minus model), in µV; mae is the mean of its absolute value;

  • baseline_sd: the size of the noise, measured before the stimulus, in µV;

  • bic and aic: scores that compare models of the same curve (lower is better); they mean nothing alone, only next to another model of the same data;

  • residual_lag1_autocorr: how much each leftover point looks like the next one. Noise of the brain is slow, so it is never 0; a value close to 1 means a slow wave is left that the model missed.

# The curve of tutorial 01, and its fit
times, signal = gen_response([[25, -150, 10], [150, -80, 80]], seed=3)
fit = fit_response(times, signal, preset="ccep", random_state=0)

gof = fit.gof
print(f"R² = {gof.r2:.2f}, rmse = {gof.rmse:.1f} µV, noise = {gof.baseline_sd:.1f} µV")
print(f"BIC = {gof.bic:.1f}, leftover lag-1 = {gof.residual_lag1_autocorr:.2f}")

R² = 0.94, rmse = 7.1 µV, noise = 7.4 µV
BIC = 33.8, leftover lag-1 = 0.78

The most useful check is to compare rmse with baseline_sd. Here the leftover is 7.1 µV and the noise 7.4 µV: the model left only noise. If the leftover were much larger than the noise, something in the curve would not be explained.

Here is the same curve, fitted with one bump at most.

fit_short = fit_response(times, signal, preset="ccep", max_components=1, random_state=0)
gof_short = fit_short.gof
print(
    f"R² = {gof_short.r2:.2f}, rmse = {gof_short.rmse:.1f} µV, noise = {gof_short.baseline_sd:.1f}"
)
print(f"leftover lag-1 = {gof_short.residual_lag1_autocorr:.2f}")

R² = 0.27, rmse = 24.1 µV, noise = 7.4
leftover lag-1 = 0.98

The leftover (24 µV) is three times the noise, and it is a slow wave (lag-1 of 0.98): the N2 is missing.

R² can mislead¶

R² compares the model with how much the curve moves. When the noise is large, even a perfect model can not explain the noise, so R² is low. And when the curve is mostly a slow background, R² is high even without any bump.

The same two bumps with more noise (30 µV instead of 8):

times_noisy, signal_noisy = gen_response([[25, -150, 10], [150, -80, 80]], noise=30, seed=3)
fit_noisy = fit_response(times_noisy, signal_noisy, preset="ccep", random_state=0)
print(f"R² = {fit_noisy.gof.r2:.2f}, rmse = {fit_noisy.gof.rmse:.1f} µV, ", end="")
print(f"noise = {fit_noisy.gof.baseline_sd:.1f} µV")
for bump in fit_noisy.components:
    print(f"  {bump.label}: {bump.latency_ms:.0f} ms, {bump.amplitude:.0f} µV")

R² = 0.48, rmse = 26.7 µV, noise = 27.9 µV
  N1: 25 ms, -159 µV
  N2: 158 ms, -73 µV

R² is only 0.48, but both bumps are found, and the leftover is the size of the noise: the fit is as good as the data allow.

And a curve with no bump at all, only the background and noise:

times_empty, signal_empty = gen_response([], seed=5)
fit_empty = fit_response(times_empty, signal_empty, preset="ccep", random_state=0)
print(f"bumps found: {len(fit_empty.components)}, R² = {fit_empty.gof.r2:.2f}")

bumps found: 0, R² = 0.67

No bump is found (right), but R² is 0.67: the fading background alone explains much of the curve. A high R² does not say that the bumps are real; a low one does not say that they are false. Compare the leftover with the noise, and look at the figure.

How sure is each number¶

Each number of a bump comes with a 95 % range (bump.ci): the values that the noise of this recording leaves possible. A wider range means a less sure number. The standard error (bump.se) is the same idea as one number, roughly a quarter of the range.

def show_ranges(fit):
    """Print each bump's latency and amplitude with their 95 % ranges."""
    for bump in fit.components:
        lat_low, lat_high = bump.ci["latency"]
        amp_low, amp_high = bump.ci["amplitude"]
        print(
            f"  {bump.label}: latency {bump.latency_ms:.1f} ms "
            f"({1000 * lat_low:.1f} to {1000 * lat_high:.1f}), "
            f"amplitude {bump.amplitude:.0f} µV ({amp_low:.0f} to {amp_high:.0f})"
        )


print("noise 8 µV")
show_ranges(fit)
print("noise 30 µV")
show_ranges(fit_noisy)

noise 8 µV
  N1: latency 25.0 ms (24.7 to 25.2), amplitude -152 µV (-164 to -140)
  N2: latency 151.9 ms (148.2 to 155.6), amplitude -77 µV (-85 to -70)
noise 30 µV
  N1: latency 24.9 ms (24.0 to 25.7), amplitude -159 µV (-201 to -117)
  N2: latency 158.0 ms (143.7 to 172.3), amplitude -73 µV (-101 to -45)

With more noise, every range widens: the N1 latency range goes from 0.5 ms to about 2 ms, the N2 latency range from 7 ms to about 29 ms. The narrow, large N1 always stays better known than the broad, small N2.

Report the range with the number: “N1 at 24.9 ms (24.0 to 25.7)” says much more than “N1 at 24.9 ms”.

Status and warnings¶

fit.status is "ok" when the fit ran normally. Otherwise it says what went wrong (for example, too little data in the window), and fit.message explains.

fit.warnings lists what the fit noticed but did not stop for. The most common:

  • "ill_conditioned": two numbers of the model are hard to tell apart (for example two bumps that overlap, or a slow background and a broad bump; tutorial 05). Their ranges are wider;

  • "at_bound:<bump>:<number>": a number stopped at its limit (for example a width at max_fwhm). The true value may be beyond it;

  • "multiple_in_window:<name>": two bumps fall in the window of one name (for example two N1 candidates); the second is called <name>_b;

  • "latency_outside_window:<bump>": a bump peaks outside the fit window, so its latency is not measured on the data.

print(fit.status, fit.warnings)

ok []

No warning here. A fit with a warning is not wrong, but it deserves a look at the figure (plot_fit) before its numbers are used.

Conclusion¶

To judge a fit: compare the leftover (rmse) with the noise (baseline_sd), do not trust R² alone, report each number with its 95 % range, and read fit.warnings. The BIC compares models of the same curve; it is what evoparam uses to choose the bumps (tutorial 03).
