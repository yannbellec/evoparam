18: Across subjects¶

Combine trials, channels and subjects in one model, with partial pooling and uncertainty.

Introduction¶

A study has three nested levels: trials inside a channel, channels inside a subject, subjects inside the group. Averaging each level in turn throws away information: a subject with two trials is trusted as much as one with two hundred, and a noisy channel counts the same as a clean one.

evoparam.hierarchical fits the three levels at once. It uses partial pooling: a noisy or sparse unit is pulled toward the group mean (shrinkage), a well-measured one is left alone. The default engine is empirical Bayes (fast, in the core); a full Bayesian engine (numpyro or pymc) is an optional extra for when you want full posteriors. The result is a group estimate with honest uncertainty, and it can use a covariate such as age.

# Import what we need
import warnings

from evoparam import plots
from evoparam.hierarchical import fit_hierarchical, posterior_summary
from evoparam.sim import simulate_ccep

The data¶

Ten subjects, five electrodes each, 20 trials. About 60 % of the electrodes answer. In a real study each subject would be one recording read with read_bids; here each is a simulated TrialSet, and we pass the list straight to fit_hierarchical.

subjects = []
for subject in range(10):
    trials, _ = simulate_ccep(
        20, n_channels=5, p_response=0.6, p_n2=1.0, artifact=False, layout="grid",
        sfreq=512.0, seed=subject, n1={"amplitude": -45.0 - 7.0 * subject},
    )  # fmt: skip
    subjects.append(trials)
print(f"{len(subjects)} subjects, {subjects[0].n_channels} channels each")

10 subjects, 5 channels each

The fit¶

fit_hierarchical fits the N1 at every level. components=["N1"] keeps the model small for the tutorial. engine="eb" is empirical Bayes, the default.

with warnings.catch_warnings():
    warnings.simplefilter("ignore")
    hier = fit_hierarchical(subjects, components=["N1"], engine="eb", random_state=0)

print(hier.methods_text())

The group estimate¶

posterior_summary reads any level. At the group level it gives one estimate per parameter, with a standard error and how many channels and subjects it rests on. This is the number a paper reports: the group N1 latency and amplitude, pooled correctly over the levels.

group_summary = posterior_summary(hier, level="group")
print(group_summary.round(3).to_string(index=False))

At the subject level it gives one row per subject. pi is the share of that subject’s channels with an N1; the latency and amplitude are the subject means, each pulled toward the group by an amount set by how well that subject is measured.

subject_summary = posterior_summary(hier, level="subject")
columns = ["subject", "n_channels_with_component", "pi", "latency", "latency_se", "amplitude"]
print(subject_summary[columns].round(3).head().to_string(index=False))

Where the variance sits¶

variance_components splits the spread of the N1 into between-subject, between-channel and between-trial parts. It answers “would more subjects or more trials help most?”: if most variance is between subjects, add subjects; if between trials, add trials. The figure shows the group estimate and the subject spread.

print(hier.variance_components.round(4).to_string(index=False))

fig = plots.plot_hierarchical(hier)

Note

A covariate goes in through a formula. fit_hierarchical(subjects, components=["N1"], formula="latency ~ age") regresses the N1 latency on each subject’s age (read from the metadata) while keeping the pooling. For full posteriors and credible intervals, engine="numpyro" or engine="pymc" (optional extras) fit the same model with MCMC; compare_hierarchical then contrasts several fits.

Conclusion¶

The hierarchical model is how you go from one recording to a group result. It pools trials, channels and subjects in one step, trusts each unit by how well it is measured, and returns a group estimate with uncertainty plus a variance breakdown that guides the next study’s design. Empirical Bayes is the fast default; the Bayesian engines are there when you need the whole posterior.
